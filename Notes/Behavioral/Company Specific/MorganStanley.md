# Morgan Stanley Behavioral Interview Prep: Core Values & Mindset

Morgan Stanley organizes its culture and interviews around five core values: **Do the Right Thing**, **Put Clients First
**, **Lead with Exceptional Ideas**, **Commit to Diversity and Inclusion**, and **Give Back**. Sitting alongside these
is the firm's cultural mindset of **Rigor, Humility, and Partnership**—how the firm expects people to think, carry
themselves, and work with others day to day.

Behavioral questions make up roughly half of all questions asked across Morgan Stanley's process—from the initial
HireVue video interview (30 seconds to think, ~2 minutes to answer) through to the final Superday/panel round.
Interviewers are explicitly listening for whether your stories map back to these five values, and whether you're
genuinely motivated by Morgan Stanley specifically (generic "I love finance" answers are easy for recruiters to spot and
filter out).

### How to Prepare

Build 6–10 strong **STAR** stories (Situation, Task, Action, Result) from work, internships, university projects, or
leadership/community activities. Most stories can be angled to demonstrate 2+ values—practice flexing the same story
toward different questions. Know the five values well enough to reference them naturally, not recite them.

## 1. General Fit & Motivation

### "Why Morgan Stanley?"

> Reported in nearly 20% of all Morgan Stanley behavioral interviews—arguably the single most important question to
> prepare.

* **Ideal Answer Approach:** Give a specific reason tied to the firm (a particular deal, research piece, strategic move,
  or division strength—not something equally true of Goldman or JPMorgan), a personal connection (networking
  conversation, project, internship), and tie it to one of the five values that genuinely resonates with you. Close with
  why this stage of your career fits their platform.

### "Walk me through your resume / Tell me about yourself."

* **Ideal Answer Approach:** A tight, chronological narrative (education → key experience → the through-line skill or
  interest) that ends by connecting naturally to why you're sitting in this interview. Keep it under 90 seconds; don't
  just restate your CV line by line.

### "Explain two things about yourself not on your résumé."

* **Ideal Answer Approach:** Pick one professional-adjacent trait (a skill or hobby that shows discipline/leadership)
  and one more personal detail that humanizes you—both should be memorable and easy for the interviewer to bring up
  later.

### "What's the most valuable advice a mentor has given you?"

* **Ideal Answer Approach:** Choose real, specific advice, briefly explain the situation that prompted it, and describe
  how you've applied it since—showing you actually act on feedback rather than just collecting quotes.

## 2. Put Clients First

> *"Keep the client's interest first and deliver the best of the firm to every client."*

### "Tell me about a time you went above and beyond for a customer or client."

* **Ideal Answer Approach:** Use a specific, concrete example (not "I always prioritize the customer"). Show you
  identified a need the client hadn't explicitly stated, took extra initiative to meet it, and quantify the result if
  possible (retained the account, resolved an issue quickly, exceeded expectations).

### "Describe a situation where you had to balance a client's needs against internal constraints (time, resources, policy)."

* **Ideal Answer Approach:** Show you didn't just cave to the client or ignore the constraint—you found or proposed a
  solution that respected both, communicating trade-offs transparently.

### "Tell me about a time you had to manage a difficult client or stakeholder relationship."

* **Ideal Answer Approach:** Lead with listening/empathy to understand their real concern, describe the specific action
  you took to resolve it, and the outcome—ideally a preserved or strengthened relationship.

---

## 3. Do the Right Thing

> *"Act with integrity and think like an owner."*

### "Tell me about a time you made a mistake. How did you handle it?"

* **Ideal Answer Approach:** Own it immediately and fully—no blame-shifting. Describe the corrective action, how
  transparently you communicated it to those affected, and the concrete lesson or process change that followed.

### "Describe a time you faced an ethical dilemma or had to do something difficult but right."

* **Ideal Answer Approach:** Show genuine tension (pressure to take a shortcut vs. the correct action), your reasoning,
  and the result—even if it came at a short-term cost. This tests integrity and courage together.

### "Tell me about a time you had to think like an owner rather than just complete a task."

* **Ideal Answer Approach:** Describe a moment you took on responsibility beyond the minimum ask—flagging a risk, fixing
  something nobody assigned you, or making a decision as if the outcome were your own money/reputation on the line.

---

## 4. Lead with Exceptional Ideas

> *"Win by breaking new ground and drive innovation using different perspectives."*

### "Tell me about a time you proposed a new idea or a better way of doing something."

* **Ideal Answer Approach:** Be specific about the old approach, your reasoning for the new one, how you got buy-in (
  especially if you faced pushback), and a measurable result.

### "Describe your greatest achievement / a project you're most proud of."

* **Ideal Answer Approach:** Pick something with a clear bar (a competition, target, deadline) that you exceeded, and be
  specific about what you uniquely contributed that drove the outcome from good to exceptional.

### "Tell me about a time you solved a complex problem with a creative or unconventional approach."

* **Ideal Answer Approach:** Walk through your problem-solving process (not just the "aha" moment)—how you broke the
  problem down, considered alternatives, and validated that the solution actually worked.

### "What business, finance, or technology trend do you think will matter over the next 12 months?"

* **Ideal Answer Approach:** Pick one trend you can speak to with real depth (not three shallow ones), connect it to a
  specific implication for Morgan Stanley's business, and offer a point of view rather than just reporting news.

---

## 5. Commit to Diversity and Inclusion

> *"Champion an environment where all employees feel a sense of belonging."*

### "Tell me about a time you worked with someone very different from you, or supported inclusion on a team."

* **Ideal Answer Approach:** Avoid abstract language like "I value diverse perspectives" without evidence. Describe a
  specific moment when you noticed a voice being left out or a decision being made with too narrow a perspective, and
  explain exactly what you did about it—and the outcome.

### "Describe a time you had to adapt your communication style to work effectively with someone."

* **Ideal Answer Approach:** Show self-awareness about a real difference (working style, seniority, cultural background,
  communication preference) and the specific adjustment you made—and why it led to a better outcome.

### "Tell me about a time you disagreed with a colleague or manager. How did you handle it?"

* **Ideal Answer Approach:** Show you disagreed respectfully and on substance, listened fully, and either reached a
  resolution or professionally deferred once a decision was made—proving you can hold a different perspective without
  damaging the relationship.

---

## 6. Give Back

> *"Building a better world means giving back and growing together."*

### "Tell me about a time you gave back to your community or supported someone else's growth."

* **Ideal Answer Approach:** Use a genuine volunteering, mentoring, or coaching example. Be specific about your role and
  the tangible outcome for the person or community, not just that you "participated."

### "Describe a time you helped develop or mentor someone junior to you."

* **Ideal Answer Approach:** Describe what they were struggling with, the concrete action you took (not just
  advice—active coaching, practice, feedback loops), and the improvement that followed.

---

## 7. Rigor, Humility & Partnership (Cross-Cutting Mindset)

### "Tell me about a time you faced a significant challenge or setback. How did you handle it?"

> One of the top 4 most commonly asked Morgan Stanley behavioral questions.

* **Ideal Answer Approach:** Own the setback honestly, focus on the specific action you took to recover (rigor), what
  you learned (humility), and how you moved forward—ideally referencing later evidence that the lesson stuck.

### "Describe a leadership moment — a time you led a team or project."

* **Ideal Answer Approach:** Even without a formal title, show how you organized, motivated, or unblocked a group, how
  you handled any resistance, and the concrete outcome.

### "Give an example of a time you worked well as part of a team, especially under pressure."

* **Ideal Answer Approach:** Emphasize partnership—how you communicated, divided work, and supported teammates—not just
  your individual contribution.

### "What is your greatest strength / weakness?"

* **Ideal Answer Approach:**
    * **Strength:** One directly relevant to the role, backed by a brief example.
    * **Weakness:** Something real and specific (not a disguised strength), paired with the concrete steps you've taken
      to improve it (humility in action).

### "Pick an athlete (or public figure) you see yourself in, and why."

* **Ideal Answer Approach:** Choose someone whose specific traits (discipline, resilience, teamwork) you can back with a
  real example from your own life—avoid a generic "they're the GOAT" answer with no personal connection.

---

## Quick Prep Checklist

* [ ] **Map Stories to Values:** Map your 6–10 stories against all five core values before the interview—most stories
  can flex to cover 2 values (e.g., a mistake-recovery story often shows both *Do the Right Thing* and *Lead with
  Exceptional Ideas*).
* [ ] **Master Openers:** Nail "Why Morgan Stanley?" and "Walk me through your resume" cold—these open almost every
  round and are asked most frequently.
* [ ] **HireVue Timings:** Rehearse on camera with the real time constraints (30 seconds to think, ~2 minutes to answer)
  so the format itself doesn't throw you off.
* [ ] **Quantify Results:** Always end answers with the Result, quantified where possible.
* [ ] **Natural Terminology:** Weave value language in naturally (e.g., *"I wanted to make sure I was putting the
  client's interest first"*) rather than announcing *"this shows Put Clients First."*
