# Barclays Behavioral Interview Prep: Values & Mindset (RISES)

Barclays builds its entire behavioral/competency interview process around one framework: RISES — Respect, Integrity,
Service, Excellence, Stewardship. These five values sit alongside a "Mindset" that Barclays describes as how we get
things done, always with customers and clients at the heart of what we do.

You'll meet this framework twice in the process:

1. **The Values & Mindset Assessment** — an untimed, ~75-question situational judgement/personality test (triplet
   forced-choice: pick the statement most like you, then least like you) that screens for RISES alignment before you
   even reach a human interviewer.
2. **The Competency/Behavioral Interview** (often HireVue video, then face-to-face/panel) — where you'll get "Tell me
   about a time..." and "Give me an example of..." questions, each mapped to one or more of the five values plus general
   fit/motivation questions.
   **How to prepare:** Have 2-3 solid stories ready (work, internship, university project, extracurricular) that you can
   flex across multiple values. Structure every answer with STAR — Situation, Task, Action, Result — and explicitly tie
   the Result back to the value being tested. Keep answers to 60-90 seconds.

### 1. General Fit & Motivation Questions

"Why Barclays?"  
What they're testing: Genuine motivation and whether you've researched the firm beyond "it's a big bank." Ideal answer
approach: Combine 3 elements — (1) something specific about Barclays' scale/history/strategy (e.g., its dual UK
consumer + global investment bank structure, or a specific division/desk), (2) a personal connection (a conversation
with an employee, a project, or product you've used), (3) a values link — e.g. you're drawn to a culture that pairs
long-term client stewardship with genuine collaboration across global teams. Avoid generic lines that could apply to any
bank.

"Why this role/division?"  
Ideal answer approach: Show you understand what the specific team actually does day-to-day (not just the job title), and
connect one or two of your skills/experiences directly to that function. End with why it fits your longer-term goals.

"What differentiates Barclays from other banks?"  
Ideal answer approach: Reference something concrete and current — e.g. its combined consumer/corporate/investment
banking model, its transatlantic presence (UK + US), or a specific piece of recent news/strategy — rather than generic "
great culture, great people" statements.

"Tell me about yourself / Walk me through your CV"  
Ideal answer approach: A tight 3-part narrative — where you've come from (education/early experience), what you've built
since (skills, one strong achievement), and why that trajectory leads naturally to this role at Barclays. Keep it under
90 seconds and end pointed at the job, not your life story.

### 2. Respect

"We harness the power of diversity and inclusion, trust those we work with, and value everyone's contribution."

"Tell me about a time you worked with someone very different from you (background, working style, seniority,
culture)."  
Ideal answer approach: Pick an example where the difference genuinely could have caused friction. Show you actively
sought their perspective rather than just tolerating it, and that the outcome was better because of that difference (
e.g. a more balanced decision, a stronger group project). Avoid stories where "respect" just means "I was polite" — show
active inclusion.

"Describe a situation where you had to consider someone else's perspective before making a decision."  
Ideal answer approach: Use STAR. Emphasize that you paused before acting, gathered input from the person/team affected,
and adjusted your approach as a result — showing humility, not just good manners.

"Give an example of a time you disagreed with a colleague or manager. How did you handle it?"  
Ideal answer approach: Show you disagreed on substance, not ego — you stated your view clearly and respectfully,
listened fully to theirs, and either reached a compromise or professionally deferred once a decision was made. Barclays
wants to see you can challenge constructively without damaging the relationship.

### 3. Integrity

"We operate with honesty, courage, transparency and fairness in all we do."

"Tell me about a time you made a mistake. How did you handle it?"  
Ideal answer approach: Own the mistake fully and quickly (no blame-shifting), explain what you did to fix it and
communicate it transparently to those affected, and end with the concrete lesson/process change that followed. This is
one of the most common Barclays questions — a story where you hid or minimized a mistake will fail here.

"Describe a time you had to do the right thing even though it was unpopular or difficult."  
Ideal answer approach: Show a genuine tension (social/commercial pressure vs. the honest/correct action), explain the
reasoning behind your choice, and the result — even if it was uncomfortable short-term. This tests courage, not just
honesty.

"Tell me about a time you saw someone else act unethically or cut corners. What did you do?"  
Ideal answer approach: If you have a real example, describe raising it through the appropriate channel calmly and
factually, without dramatizing. If you don't have a direct example, pivot to a hypothetical grounded in Barclays' Code
of Conduct: escalate through the correct line, stay factual, protect the client/firm's interest.

### 4. Service

"We act with empathy and humility, putting the people and businesses we serve at the centre of what we do."

"Tell me about a time you went above and beyond for a customer, client, or team member."  
Ideal answer approach: Choose a specific, measurable example (not "I always try my best"). Show you identified an
unstated need, took initiative beyond your basic responsibility, and quantify the impact if possible (time saved,
satisfaction improved, problem prevented).

"Describe a situation where you had to manage a difficult or dissatisfied customer/stakeholder."  
Ideal answer approach: Lead with empathy — show you listened to understand the root frustration before defending or
explaining. Then show the concrete action you took to resolve it, and the outcome (retained relationship, resolved
complaint).

"Give an example of when you had to balance the needs of multiple stakeholders."  
Ideal answer approach: Show you didn't just pick a winner — you found a way to understand each stakeholder's real
priority and structured a solution (or clear trade-off communication) that kept the end client/customer's interest
central.

### 5. Excellence

"We set high standards, championing innovation and using our energy, expertise and resources to make a positive
difference."

"Tell me about a time you improved a process or found a more efficient way of doing something."  
Ideal answer approach: Be specific about the old way vs. your new way, the reasoning/data behind the change, and a
measurable result (time, cost, quality). This is a strong one to have quantified — Barclays likes numbers.

"Describe your greatest achievement / a time you delivered exceptional results."  
Ideal answer approach: Pick something with a clear bar you exceeded (a target, a deadline, a competition), be honest
about the difficulty, and show what specifically you did that pushed the result from "good" to "excellent."

"Tell me about a time you had to learn something new quickly to complete a task."  
Ideal answer approach: Show a structured approach to learning (not panic), the specific action taken to get up to speed,
and that you still delivered to a high standard under the time pressure.

"What is your greatest strength / weakness?"  
Ideal answer approach: Strength — pick one directly relevant to the role and back it with a brief example. Weakness —
choose something real and specific (not a disguised strength like "I work too hard"), and show the concrete steps you've
taken to actively improve it.

### 6. Stewardship

"We are passionate about leaving things better than we found them" — long-term thinking, protecting the firm's
reputation, sustainability.

"Tell me about a time you thought about the long-term impact of a decision, rather than just the short-term result."  
Ideal answer approach: Show a moment where the "easy" short-term option existed but you chose (or recommended) the
option that was better for the organization, team, or client over time — and explain the reasoning transparently.

"Describe a time you took ownership of something beyond your direct responsibility, to leave things better than you
found them."  
Ideal answer approach: A genuine "above the job description" example — fixing a broken process, mentoring a junior
colleague, flagging a risk nobody had noticed. Emphasize that no one asked you to do it.

"How do you think about risk management / protecting a firm's reputation in your work?"  
Ideal answer approach: Even without direct banking experience, show a mindset of "check twice, escalate early, don't cut
corners for speed." Use an academic/work example where you caught or flagged a risk before it became a problem.

### 7. Leadership, Teamwork & Resilience (cross-cutting, commonly asked)

"Tell me about a time you led a team or project."  
Ideal answer approach: Even without a formal title, describe a moment you took initiative to organize, motivate, or
unblock a group. Focus on how you got buy-in and handled any resistance, and the concrete outcome.

"Describe a time you supported a colleague's development."  
Ideal answer approach: A specific coaching/mentoring moment — what they were struggling with, what you did (not just
advice, but action/practice/feedback), and the improvement that followed. Barclays explicitly tests this one for
analyst-level roles.

"Tell me about a time you faced a setback or failure. How did you respond?"  
Ideal answer approach: Own the failure honestly, don't over-explain excuses, show the specific behavior change or
lesson, and ideally reference a later situation where that lesson paid off.

"Describe a time you had to manage competing priorities/deadlines."  
Ideal answer approach: Show a clear method (triage by impact/urgency, transparent communication with stakeholders about
trade-offs) rather than just "I worked longer hours."

### Quick Prep Checklist

Map 3-4 of your strongest stories against all five RISES values before the interview — most stories can flex to cover 2
values (e.g., a mistake-recovery story often shows both Integrity and Excellence).  
Always close with the Result, and where possible quantify it.  
Have one clear, specific answer ready for "Why Barclays" and "Why this role" — these open almost every interview.  
For the online Values & Mindset assessment: when in doubt between two statements, pick the one that shows humility,
collaboration, and long-term thinking over individual credit or short-term wins.
